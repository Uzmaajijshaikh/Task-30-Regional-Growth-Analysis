Data Analytics Internship – Task 30 (Regional Growth Analysis)

This repository contains my submission for Task 30 of the Data Analytics Internship.

Dataset - Sample Superstore Dataset

Objective - The objective was to compare regional sales growth over time using period-over-period comparisons and identify changes in regional performance.

Tools Used
1. DB Browser for SQLite
2. Microsoft Excel
3. GitHub

Regional Growth Analysis
1. Used the Sample Superstore Dataset containing sales data from 2014 to 2017.
2. Analyzed sales performance across the Central, East, South, and West regions.
3. Calculated yearly sales for each region using SQL.
4. Compared sales between consecutive years to calculate period-over-period growth.
5. Analyzed growth for the periods 2014–2015, 2015–2016, and 2016–2017.
6. Created a regional growth table in Excel.
7. Created a line chart to visualize growth across the three periods.
8. Compared positive and negative growth across regions.
9. Identified regions showing strong recovery after periods of decline.
10. Considered the small-base effect when interpreting growth percentages.
11. Created five key insights based on the regional growth analysis.

Key Results
1. Central Growth - 2014–15: -0.93%, 2015–16: 43.31%, 2016–17: -0.22%
2. East Growth - 2014–15: 21.49%, 2015–16: 15.58%, 2016–17: 17.93%
3. South Growth - 2014–15: -31.28%, 2015–16: 31.18%, 2016–17: 31.30%
4. West Growth - 2014–15: -5.35%, 2015–16: 33.95%, 2016–17: 33.42%
5. Highest Single-Period Growth - Central recorded 43.31% growth during 2015–2016.

Key Insights
1. East maintained consistent positive growth across all three periods.
2. South recorded the largest decline of 31.28% in 2014–15 but recovered strongly in the following two periods.
3. West recovered from a 5.35% decline and achieved more than 33% growth in both following periods.
4. Central recorded the highest single-period growth of 43.31% in 2015–16 but remained almost flat in the other two periods.
5. Growth rates can be misleading because a low starting value can result in a high percentage increase, which is known as the small-base effect.
